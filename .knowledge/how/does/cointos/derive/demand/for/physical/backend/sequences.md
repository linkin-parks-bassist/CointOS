Source `backend_profile_policy.model_demand(jobs, model_id, route_for_job)` counts `runner_starting` and `running` managed tasks against their exact assigned physical `model`, without rerouting them. It freshly routes `queued`, `ready`, and uncancelled `claimed` jobs; a `claimed` record may still carry its previous model because `execute_next` persists the claim before refreshing the route. A valid nonempty-string cancellation marker excludes that pre-admission claim. Only a valid selected route contributes unbound demand. This prevents stale claimed/ready model fields from requesting growth for the wrong resident backend while preserving physically bound demand. The claimed-route correction is installed without a service restart; three existing pure tests cover ready routing, active binding, and invalid-route exclusion, a bounded direct check covers claimed rerouting and cancellation, and standard discovery passes 837 tests. Live qualification remains pending.

`backend_profile_scheduler.assess_profiles()` reads resident health, per-sequence allocation, durable demand, a fingerprinted qualified ceiling, generic work-slot policy and fresh incremental KV headroom. It selects at most one model-neutral target per pass, delays shrink for `profile_minimum_dwell_seconds`, grows immediately when qualified demand returns, and cools down failed targets for `profile_failure_cooldown_seconds`. Eligible growth takes precedence over eligible shrink across resident models, so alphabetical model order cannot delay useful capacity behind an idle reload. A model waiting on shrink dwell or target-failure cooldown does not prevent another eligible resident model from being selected in the same pass; if no model can change, the first deferred reason is returned. Fenced recovery takes precedence.

The installed timer calls `cointos-profile reconcile` about once a minute and automation is enabled. A live two-job CointOS-only probe made read-only `cointos-profile plan` report demand two/target two from a one-sequence profile; the timer grew it to two, both workers held distinct physical sequences and completed exit 0 with leases released. That growth revealed a delayed executor wake, now repaired in the installed generation; a later automatic idle shrink triggered immediate ecosystem dispatch and cleared the durable wake marker. The exact Qwen3.8 ceiling of two is runtime evidence, not a hardcoded named-model value; unloaded work models conservatively start at one. Demand is an input to physical capacity, not a guarantee of instant simultaneous service. The cross-model routing/counting and growth-before-shrink ordering are backend-free tested but still await live multi-model qualification.
