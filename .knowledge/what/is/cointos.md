CointOS is a personal, local-first autonomous software factory. It runs continuously on the user's workstation and uses local GPU capacity to deliver checked results without turn-by-turn direction. Its four governing requirements are:

1. **Coin is responsive, capable and always reachable.** Coin is the user's control surface, with broad authorised local capabilities and durable conversation, intake and result delivery. Factory load, a model failure or a maintenance operation must not silently strand the user. Coin answers promptly and delegates substantial work while remaining available.
2. **Work proceeds autonomously without user oversight.** Agents discover approved work, plan, implement, test, review, integrate and recover it. They preserve requirements and artifacts, resolve ordinary engineering decisions themselves and leave verified results. The user supplies intent and consequential authority, rather than repeatedly checking progress or telling agents to continue.
3. **Workflow blockage and wasted GPU time are minimised.** Detect and resolve stalled handoffs, dead dependencies, ineffective recovery, repeated investigation, avoidable rereading and idle capacity. GPU activity alone is not useful progress. Measure outcomes, charged work, waiting, tools and cache overhead honestly.
4. **Scheduling creates the experience of sustained parallel work.** More agents than physical model lanes remain alive and make recurring progress over meaningful time. Coin/Operators receive very high service priority, First Responders receive prompt recovery service, and ordinary work still progresses. No strict tier may exclude lower-priority work indefinitely. Reading, generation and switching costs count; waiting and tools do not.

The desktop and the user's own work remain responsive. Product work lands through independent tests/review and checked receipts in ordinary repositories. Tasks, sessions, branches, accepted refs and outward evidence survive interruption. Only the daemon writes runtime authority and queue projections; knowledge trees own maintained answers.

CointOS's own maintenance must follow the same autonomous loop: detect a problem, make a scoped change, deploy safely, observe for the required period, evaluate actual effects and repair/roll back or continue verification. Green checks, a deployment receipt or an intention to watch later are not completion. Existing scouts, stewards, supervisors and recovery must observe outcomes and arrange follow-up without the user repeatedly prompting; use the existing durable work and event paths. The self-development contract owns this requirement and its remaining implementation gaps.

Simplicity, non-interference with live inhabitants and professional/private boundaries remain binding. First Responders own canonical engineering recovery; unavailable-runtime recovery needs an independent route outside the failed local gateway. Source implementation and installed acceptance are distinct. Broader Sole Survivor coverage remains excluded.
