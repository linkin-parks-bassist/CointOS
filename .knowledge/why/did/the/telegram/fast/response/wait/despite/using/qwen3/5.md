The delayed Telegram front responses were scheduler waits rather than slow Qwen3.5
decode. The `yippie` turn spent about 43 seconds retrying admission 16 times with
`sequence_unavailable`, then generated and delivered in about 2.8 seconds. Later
front runs showed the same shorter retry pattern while live Qwen3.5 `/slots` showed
two idle sequences and CointOS had no active inference leases.

The cause was the meaning assigned to `front_slots = 1`. Capacity admission treated
that reservation as both a guaranteed front sequence and a maximum: front requests
searched only sequence 0, while sequence 1 could remain idle. A front reservation is
now a minimum guarantee. Front requests prefer reserved sequences, then spill into
any idle general sequence, and may preempt lower-priority work when all sequences are
occupied. Background work still cannot consume the reserved front range. Model-level
front concurrency is bounded by observed backend parallel capacity rather than the
reserved minimum. A focused check proves two concurrent Coin front requests receive
sequences 0 and 1 under a one-front/one-work configuration.
